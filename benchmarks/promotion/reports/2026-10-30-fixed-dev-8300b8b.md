## Protocol

Model: grok-4.7, reasoning effort xhigh, through Cursor cloud-agent file handoff into a loopback chat server on `127.0.0.1`. No xAI API key and no other model API. Each arm has its own loopback, because the server indexes the SHA-256 of the user message and the two baseline arms share those bytes while holding different answers.

Locked rank `forecast-k1`, coefficient set `apr-fusion-v1`: `--cheap --holistic-mode forecast --holistic-samples 1 --related-work off`. The rank is `rank_score`, the mean of the three seat expected ratings. The venue bin is the 1–6 decision from the same call.

Pre-release, the tail candidate, and the accept-anchor candidate were scored with `calibrate-eval run --fixed-set iclr-dev --model-id grok-4.7 --effort xhigh` and those flags. Main's CLI at `3865669` has no `--fixed-set` flag, so main was scored with main's `src` and the same adapter, model, loopback, and forecast flags, on a cache that contains exactly these 48 papers. The pre-release harness then computed the metrics. `ForecastJudgment` accepted every scored answer. Parse-failure count is 0 on every scored arm. Pipeline-error count is 0. Each paper used one call. Scored call count is 192.

The pre-release and main user messages match on all 48 papers. Forty-six are uncut and two are cut at the 240000-character cap. `forecast_task_text()` SHA-256 on the baseline is `4374696de5c606470815b72c53ac5382afbeab79501f651c86608241fe1800b7` (6780 characters). On the kept accept-anchor candidate it is `3611121d0cee15e7f503e2dd90557bbbcaf4b3ea48f487b5cd7ba127a53889e4` (7229 characters).

The decision intervals are the paired stratified bootstrap: one draw applied to both arms, strata restored with replacement in sorted stratum order, 4000 resamples, seed `20261029`, linear percentiles 0.025 and 0.975. All 4000 resamples were defined on every comparison below. A higher-is-better metric is worse beyond the interval when the whole interval is below zero. MAE is worse beyond the interval when the whole interval is above zero. An interval that contains zero is unchanged for this rule. The single-arm intervals further down are a different bootstrap: paper-level, 4000 resamples, seed `20261021`, from `scripts/rank_eval.py`. They are the marginal uncertainty of one arm. They are not the promotion intervals.

## Attempt log

Four full dev-set runs were scored. This round used one of the seven allowed further runs. A discarded cloud batch could not read the prepared prompts and is not in the table. There is no separate meter. The earlier three runs were about $15 at the stated about $5 a run.

2. **Main.** Same 48 papers, same flags, main's source, separate answers. Prompt bytes match the baseline on every paper. Under the promotion rule, pre-release is not worse than main on any primary metric. Rank point estimates sit slightly on main's side, inside intervals that contain zero. Bin agreement favors pre-release, and that interval lies above zero.
3. **Aggregation sweep on the baseline judgments.** No new model call. The locked rank was compared with chair mean, vote mean, the high seat, the low seat, the median of the three seat expectations, and a confidence-weighted mean of those expectations. On the baseline judgments the alternatives were: chair Spearman 0.6644 / AUC 0.8080 / MAE 1.1885; vote 0.6352 / 0.7982 / 1.1691; high seat 0.6738 / 0.7937 / 1.3251; low seat 0.6214 / 0.7920 / 1.3237; median 0.6566 / 0.8277 / 1.1583; confidence-weighted 0.6519 / 0.8071 / 1.1594. Locked forecast-k1 is 0.6442 / 0.8063 / 1.1659. The largest shifts are a few hundredths, and the sweep reads seven scores off the same 48 papers. None was promoted. The same sweep on the tail judgments was inspected after that run and was also left unused: picking the best of those scores after seeing them would fit this set.
4. **Tail-anchor prompt.** One paragraph appended to `forecast_task_text()` only. It tells a cite-level review to put most of its probability on 8, a decisive reject to put most of its probability on 3, and `predicted_mean` to sit near those anchors. Shared scale paragraphs and the other review modes were left unchanged. The candidate task text is 7389 characters, SHA-256 `599d1d453accefa88af4a2a3954afec8f896f7214cd699b863919236867907c6`. All 48 user messages differ from the baseline by that paragraph. The run scored 48 of 48 through the loopback, with 0 parse failures. Spearman and AUC point estimates move up, and every primary paired interval against the baseline contains zero. Clear-reject probability at or below 3 moves from 0.441 to 0.530, and the tier mean moves from 4.361 to 4.019. Clear-accept probability at or above 8 moves from 0.282 to 0.333, and the tier mean stays at 5.97. Borderline-accept versus clear-accept AUC moves from 0.556 to 0.493. The paragraph was removed after this measurement.
5. **Stored-score affine map, then the accept-anchor prompt.** Before another model call, an affine map `a * rank_score + b` was fit on the stored baseline scores. Four folds, one from each tier in each fold, fit on the other three folds, predictions pooled. Held-out Spearman 0.6443, Kendall 0.4741, AUC 0.8143, MAE 1.1726, against the unmapped baseline 0.6442 / 0.4752 / 0.8063 / 1.1659. The held-out clear-accept mean is 6.415, still far from 7.875. A monotone map cannot restore a top end the model did not separate, so the next change is in the prompt. The baseline gloss said an 8 is a rating the reviewer would cite. On clear-accept papers the modal seat rating was 6. The kept text says an 8 is an ordinary accept, a 6 is only just above the line, and `predicted_mean` for a paper the reviewers would accept sits near 8 rather than in the 5.6–6.4 band. Shared scale paragraphs and the other review modes were left unchanged. The run scored 48 of 48 through the loopback, with 0 parse failures.

Stopping here for this round. The rank intervals still contain zero, so a further wording pass aimed only at Spearman would be fitting noise. The kept change is the one that moves the accept tail and clears two calibration intervals.
